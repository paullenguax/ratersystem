A

First time at the console

Ten things to know before your first sitting, in the order you'll actually meet them.

  1. Two windows, one sitting Fig. 2Fig. 8

    Your screen runs the script and the recordings. A separate candidate window — a pop-up you open with one click — shows the candidate their pictures and question lists. The small monitor icon in your header fills in solid the moment that window is open, and keeps checking every second, so it never lies to you about whether the candidate can actually see anything.

  2. Read the header like an instrument panel Fig. 2

    Left to right: a progress label (Slide 14/28), the overall test clock, the current Part's clock, the current slide's prep/response clock, a volume slider that controls everything played from your machine, the candidate-window icon, and Notes.

  3. Accept or reject the test Fig. 1

    The first screen names the test and asks you to Accept or Reject it. Reject asks you to confirm, ends the sitting immediately, and is reported — there's no silent way to back out of a bad match.

  4. Confirm the booking, don't type it Fig. 6

    Centre, Test Number, Examiner and Candidate name are shown read-only, pulled straight from the real booking. Tick I agree to abide by Lenguax terms once you've checked they match the person in front of you — this step exists to catch a mismatch, not to collect data.

  5. Work the room-setup checklist Fig. 5

    Tick each item as you complete it. Two carry their own button: 🖥 opens or focuses the candidate window for you, 🔊 plays the volume-check clip on the spot. Next stays locked until every box is ticked.

  6. Wait for the media to finish caching Fig. 4

    Before the timer can start, every recording and photo in the whole test is pulled into memory — a bar in the header tracks it. If something can't be fetched you're told exactly what, with a Retry or an explicit Start without them — never a silent gap mid-test.

  7. Blue italic is spoken, black upright is for you Fig. 3

    Script lines render in royal-blue italic — read those aloud. Anything upright and black, like "Invite the candidate into the room", is a stage direction: something to do, never to say.

  8. Play, pause, and mind the tick marks Fig. 3

    Each recording gets its own Play / Pause / Stop. Where a clip has a play limit you'll see ticks and a count, e.g. ✓✓ 2/2 plays. At the limit, Play greys out — a small ↻ re-arms exactly one more play, and that click is logged, so only reach for it when you mean to.

  9. Notes stay docked beside you Fig. 7

    Examiner-only guidance for the current slide sits in a panel on the right by default. Hide Notes tucks it away if it's in your way; the header's Notes button brings it straight back.

  10. Finishing up Fig. 9

    Next turns into ✓ Finish on the last slide. An Event Log at the very bottom timestamps everything that happened, for your own reference after the door closes.

B

Returning interlocutors — what's changed

For anyone who already ran candidates through the old Storyline files. Same job, mostly the same habits — here's exactly what's different underneath.

Still exactly what you remember

  • The green "candidate window is open" indicator — same idea, now a single icon in the header instead of a separate check.
  • The footer Event Log — still there, still timestamped, still just for your own reference.
  • Recordings still play through your own speakers into the room — nothing about the physical setup changed.
  1. Media loads before you can start Fig. 4

    A caching bar appears before Start Test unlocks. A dropped connection mid-test can no longer strand a Part 3 recording or a Part 4 photo — everything's already sitting in memory.

  2. Play limits show their working Fig. 3

    Ticks and an X/Y count sit right on the button now, not just a greyed-out state. Going past the limit takes a deliberate ↻ click — and that click is logged for review, so it's not a quiet workaround anymore.

  3. The checklist does two jobs now Fig. 5

    Same tick-boxes, but 🖥 and 🔊 buttons sit right on the relevant rows — open the candidate window or play the volume check without leaving the slide to go hunting for a separate control.

  4. Test Data confirm is read-only Fig. 6

    Centre, Test Number, Examiner and Candidate name come straight from the booking. You're checking them against the room, not re-typing them.

  5. Prep and Response are two separate, automatic clocks

    Prep counts down and can hand straight off to Response. Response counts up and turns red past the guideline — but keeps running, since a lower-level candidate may legitimately be given longer.

  6. More gets reported centrally now

    Closing the candidate window mid-test, or replaying a maxed-out recording, both get flagged automatically to a compliance address. Nothing to do differently — just worth knowing it's no longer only the paper record.

  7. You can rehearse whenever you like below

    The Training run is a full dress-rehearsal sitting, dressed up exactly like a real one, that you can open with no real booking behind it. Click through it as many times as it takes to feel at home.
